# 2026-09-30 GH1 pilot: BRENDA cross-reference and negative-comment audit

## Question

Does a source-cell-linked non-detection from the GH1 NIMS pilot appear in the public enzyme resource BRENDA, and should database comparison search only explicit binary labels?

Relevance: **3/3** to determining whether literature-derived negative evidence is incrementally available beyond existing enzyme resources.

## One observation checked

The first row of the extracted sample, `GH1NIMS0001`, is the Heins et al. (2014) observation for GH1 accession `AAU43012.1` from *Bacillus licheniformis* DSM 13 (= ATCC 14580), xylobiose, pH 5, 40 °C, reported as `<0.1` conversion against the paper's stated 0.1 background. The underlying SI source cell is Table 2 `D119`; the sample CSV retains the file, worksheet, row, and cell.

### Important correction to the first pass

The first pass incorrectly compared this protein against generic beta-glucosidase EC `3.2.1.21` based on the paper's family-level language. Fetching the NCBI GenBank record for [AAU43012.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/protein/AAU43012.1) shows the product description “family 1 glycoside hydrolase” and an explicit feature qualifier `EC_number=3.2.1.86` (6-phospho-beta-glucosidase). The BRENDA *B. licheniformis* species page includes `3.2.1.86` among its 129 EC entries. Thus, for this sample, the **species + NCBI EC annotation is present at BRENDA's taxon page level**; the earlier “species + EC non-match” using `3.2.1.21` was the wrong comparison and is superseded. It still does not establish an exact BRENDA protein accession match or whether the `AAU43012.1` × xylobiose × pH 5 × 40 °C `<0.1` observation is represented.

## Why a simple “BRENDA is positive-only” assumption is wrong

BRENDA's beta-glucosidase (`EC 3.2.1.21`) records can contain explicit negative substrate statements in the `Substrates and Products` commentary. For example, its record for the metagenome-derived enzyme from an uncultured bacterium includes “no hydrolysis” for xylan, cellopentaose, cellohexaose, and carboxymethylcellulose; the same entry separately records cellobiose as a substrate with low activity. The corresponding enzyme page also records “no activity with” a beta-xylopyranoside analogue. These are contextual, enzyme-specific observations embedded in commentary, not necessarily a normalized table of tested-positive and tested-negative pairs.

Consequences for our research:

1. Do not say that all negative enzyme–substrate evidence is absent from BRENDA.
2. Do not interpret the absence of a substrate row as evidence that an assay was performed and was negative; it may be untested, uncurated, or represented only in narrative commentary.
3. A database-coverage audit must search (a) enzyme/organism/accession cross-references, (b) structured substrates/products, and (c) free-text commentary for negative and low-activity statements, while retaining match confidence and exact source references.
4. Use each protein record's own EC annotation where available; do not assign `3.2.1.21` to every GH1 candidate. The NIMS example has a species+EC annotation hit (`3.2.1.86`) but its exact tested-negative observation remains unresolved.

## Sample-level BRENDA/NCBI crosswalk

The 100-row pilot contains 38 distinct protein labels: 34 distinct versioned GenBank protein accessions and four culture/metagenomic labels without a GenBank protein accession. All 34 accession records were retrieved from NCBI E-utilities. Using the GenBank organism field to resolve source labels where possible, a serialized query confirmed 30 BRENDA binomial-species pages; six protein labels covering 23 observations remained unresolved at this level. This is a correction to the first-pass total of 26 unqueryable observations.

Across the 34 accession records, 14 had an explicit NCBI `/EC_number` feature qualifier. Of these, 12 had a confirmed BRENDA binomial-species page; literal comparison with those species-page EC lists yielded 3 species+EC intersections and 9 with no intersection. Two EC-annotated accessions had no confirmed corresponding BRENDA species page. This is not an exact protein identity join: BRENDA page is species-level, GenBank functional annotations vary in specificity, synonyms/taxonomic renames can prevent literal joins, and EC-list co-occurrence does not mean that the NIMS observation is in BRENDA.

The fixed `EC 3.2.1.21` query produced 4/30 species pages with that EC, but it is **not** a valid coverage estimate for the whole GH1 sample: the paper's GH family includes diverse product annotations. For example, among those four species-page hits, the NCBI descriptions include a beta-galactosidase (ADG73989.1, *Cellulomonas flavigena*), a beta-glucosidase (BAA74959.1, *Trichoderma reesei*), a beta-glucosidase with a changed organism name (BAB05642.1, *Halalkalibacterium halodurans*), and a family-level GH1 entry with EC 3.2.1.21 (ACL70277.1, *Halothermothrix orenii*). Those are not interchangeable exact matches. The accession-level join and a standalone deterministic comparison script are preserved in [`ncbi_brenda_species_ec_join.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/ncbi_brenda_species_ec_join.csv) and [`compare_species_ec_crosswalk.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/compare_species_ec_crosswalk.py); refresh upstream data with [`audit_ncbi_accessions.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_ncbi_accessions.py) followed by [`audit_brenda_sample_taxa.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_brenda_sample_taxa.py), then rerun the join script.

## Limited page-level scan for negative wording

To test the next retrieval step, the 12 BRENDA organism pages corresponding to the 14 EC-annotated accessions with a confirmed species page were fetched again and searched with a small, explicit phrase list (e.g. “no activity”, “no hydrolysis”, “not active”). One page-level candidate was found on the *Enterococcus faecalis* page: “no activity in strain IAM 10067”, adjacent to BRENDA literature identifiers 2075, 2077, and 2078. Following the cited BRENDA entry resolves it to EC `5.1.1.13` (aspartate racemase), not the pilot accession `CBL32986.1` (EC `3.2.1.86`, GH1) or its NIMS assay. The same BRENDA record includes the substrate-level comment “no racemase activity with Glu.” Thus, this is a **confirmed false match for the pilot row**, but a direct example that BRENDA can represent a negative substrate statement for a different enzyme from the same species. It is not counted as sample-row coverage. The other 11 pages had no hit for this limited phrase set, which is not evidence that their records contain no negative observations.

This small scan demonstrates why simple organism-page keyword search has both value and a serious false-match risk: the hit is genuinely negative evidence in BRENDA, but for an unrelated enzyme/substrate. Each hit must be linked to an enzyme, tested substrate, and source reference before it enters an observation-level coverage count. The candidate and surrounding visible text are retained in [`brenda_negative_commentary_candidates.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/brenda_negative_commentary_candidates.csv); the query can be repeated with [`scan_brenda_negative_commentary.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/scan_brenda_negative_commentary.py).

## First exact-identifier pass through NCBI UniProt cross-references

The GenBank crosswalk contains four explicit `UniProtKB` cross-references among the 34 sample accessions. The official UniProt flat-file endpoint returned a parseable record for one: `D4LC32`, linked from GenBank accession `CBL17177.1`, with a 444-aa sequence and EC `3.2.1.21`. Its UniProt name is beta-glucosidase, while the linked GenBank definition says beta-galactosidase—an annotation discrepancy that must not be silently harmonized. An independent exact-accession cross-check on the [CAZy *Ruminococcus champanellensis* page](https://www.cazy.org/b1171.html) identifies `CBL17177.1` as beta-glucosidase `RUM_10120`; the [CAZyme3D entry](https://pro.unl.edu/CAZyme3D/get_entry.php?cazyid=CBL17177.1) maps it to D4LC32 and EC `3.2.1.21`, with beta-glucan as its substrate annotation. This supports beta-glucosidase as the cross-resource assignment, but the CAZyme3D substrate field is not evidence that the Heins NIMS negative assay tested beta-glucan. The BRENDA *Ruminococcus champanellensis* species page lists only EC `4.2.1.10`, not `3.2.1.21`, and does not show `D4LC32` in its visible text. The other three UniProt requests returned an empty response body in this run; this is recorded as unresolved retrieval, not as deleted or nonexistent proteins.

The public [BRENDA amino-acid sequence search](https://www.brenda-enzymes.org/sequences.php) provides a direct exact-accession route that the earlier organism-page scan missed. Querying all four UniProt IDs linked from the sample's NCBI records returned exact sequence-index records for two: `D4LC32` (linked to sample accession `CBL17177.1`; EC 3.2.1.21, beta-glucosidase, *R. champanellensis*, 444 aa) and `A0ACM7` (linked to `CAJ88232.1`; EC 3.2.1.21, beta-glucosidase, *Streptomyces ambofaciens*, 424 aa). The exact queries returned no matching sequence row for `Q55000` (`CAA52344.1`) or `D4MF92` (`CBL32986.1`). Across these four accession-linked proteins only, the two exact sequence hits correspond to 8 of 12 pilot observations. **This is sequence-index coverage, not measured-negative-observation coverage.** In particular, the BRENDA sequence records provide enzyme/sequence metadata; no match has yet been shown for the pilot's xylobiose/lactose/cellobiose result at its pH and temperature, or for the Heins source-cell observation itself.

This exact-index result supersedes the earlier narrower statement that `D4LC32` was absent from BRENDA based on its species-page visible text. The species-page query did not expose the sequence record; the dedicated sequence index does. It remains a limited check because only 4/34 NCBI accessions had a UniProt cross-reference and sequence-index hits do not establish BRENDA assay curation. The underlying rows and exact query URLs are retained in [`brenda_sequence_index_hits.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/brenda_sequence_index_hits.csv); rerun with [`query_brenda_sequence_accessions.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/query_brenda_sequence_accessions.py). Earlier UniProt flat-file responses remain in [`uniprot_brenda_accession_audit.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/uniprot_brenda_accession_audit.csv), generated by [`audit_uniprot_brenda_accessions.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_uniprot_brenda_accessions.py).

The two sequence hits were subsequently cross-checked against the original NCBI FASTA sequence for each sample GenBank accession: `CBL17177.1` equals BRENDA `D4LC32` at 444/444 residues, and `CAJ88232.1` equals BRENDA `A0ACM7` at 424/424 residues. The first parsing attempt appeared to find a four-residue mismatch in the latter because the BRENDA display's last row (`420 ARAG`) was shorter than the parser's minimum length; after including that terminal row, both pairs matched exactly. Reproduce with [`audit_brenda_sequence_identity.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_brenda_sequence_identity.py); results: [`brenda_sequence_identity_audit.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/brenda_sequence_identity_audit.csv). This confirms the protein links but does not upgrade sequence-index coverage into assay/negative-observation coverage.

An attempt to broaden this to all 34 GenBank accessions by submitting their full NCBI amino-acid sequences to the BRENDA exact-sequence form did **not** produce usable results: for a known 444-aa sequence (CBL17177.1 / D4LC32), GET and POST through Python and GET through PowerShell each ended with an incomplete response. NCBI sequence retrieval itself succeeded. This is an interface/transport failure, not a BRENDA non-match; the 34-accession exact-sequence query remains unresolved. A reproducible draft is retained as [`audit_brenda_exact_sequence_coverage.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_brenda_exact_sequence_coverage.py), but it has only passed syntax compilation and a single failed-transport smoke test, not a full run.

## Public SPARQL knowledge-graph coverage check

BRENDA announced a first public SPARQL knowledge-graph prototype in September 2025. Its announcement describes entities including enzymes linked to UniProt, reactions, organisms, and references, and reports 10,798,737 RDF triples at initial release. The endpoint responded to this audit's class/predicate and organism queries. An exact `hasIdentifier` join against the four UniProt IDs found in the GH1 pilot returned **0/4 graph entities** (D4LC32, A0ACM7, Q55000, D4MF92). The same graph did return a *R. champanellensis* enzyme linked to UniProt D4LCT8 and EC 4.2.1.10; this is a different protein from sample CBL17177.1 / D4LC32 and is retained as an explicit near-match warning, not as sample coverage.

This zero is limited to the prototype graph's exact UniProt identifier edges. It does not contradict the classic BRENDA sequence-index hits for D4LC32 and A0ACM7 above, nor show that classic BRENDA enzyme tables or source papers lack observations. The prototype predicates inspected did not expose a dedicated assay-outcome relation, so SPARQL did not resolve any NIMS substrate × pH × temperature negative observation. Reproduce the query with [`audit_brenda_sparql_identifiers.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_brenda_sparql_identifiers.py); its row-level outcomes are in [`brenda_sparql_identifier_audit.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/brenda_sparql_identifier_audit.csv).

## Attempt to retrieve classic enzyme-level references

The classic enzyme page was queried using combined EC, organism, and UniProt filters. The initial static HTML showed a JavaScript-required notice and an empty References shell, which was correctly treated as unresolved (E044). I then ran the pages with headless Edge under fresh temporary profiles so client-side JavaScript could finish. For both exact records—D4LC32 in *R. champanellensis* and A0ACM7 in *S. ambofaciens*—the rendered page confirmed the target filter, but exposed **zero** Substrates/Products rows, **zero** Natural Substrates rows, and **zero** References entries. The row-level counts are in [`brenda_js_filtered_tables_audit.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/brenda_js_filtered_tables_audit.csv); reproduce with [`audit_brenda_js_filtered_tables.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_brenda_js_filtered_tables.py).

This is a bounded, protein-filtered BRENDA non-match for the specific activity tables—not proof that BRENDA has no information under aliases/other EC assignments, or that the source papers are absent from BRENDA's broader literature index. The two proteins account for eight explicit `<0.1` source observations (seven on CBL17177.1: five xylobiose, one cellobiose, one lactose; one lactose on CAJ88232.1). These are now “not found in the exact protein-filtered BRENDA substrate/reference view,” not yet proven literature-unique observations. No database-wide uniqueness percentage is claimed.

## Bulk exact-sequence expansion and enzyme-table follow-up

To extend beyond the four GenBank records with explicit UniProt cross-references, I downloaded BRENDA's all-sequence FASTA collections for the four EC classes explicitly present in the pilot's NCBI qualifiers: EC 3.2.1.21 (32,091 sequences), 3.2.1.23 (63,272), 3.2.1.85 (1,526), and 3.2.1.86 (8,988), 105,877 entries total. Exact sequence comparison with all 34 versioned GenBank protein accessions found 21 matches, representing 62 of 86 sample observations tied to those accession labels. Of the 14 accessions with an explicit NCBI EC qualifier, 12 matched a BRENDA sequence in the same EC set, covering 41 of 48 observations for this annotated subset. Thirteen accessions (24 observations) had no exact sequence hit in these four downloaded EC sets; this does **not** exclude matches under other EC numbers or annotations. The reproducible result table is [`brenda_ec_sequence_all_sample_audit.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/brenda_ec_sequence_all_sample_audit.csv), generated by [`audit_brenda_ec_sequence_bulk.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_brenda_ec_sequence_bulk.py).

I then filtered a classic BRENDA enzyme page for each exact sequence-matched BRENDA entry by its own EC, organism, and UniProt accession, executing page JavaScript in fresh temporary headless Edge profiles. **Count correction:** an earlier narrative summary said the 24 initial page rows represented 21 sample accessions / 62 observations. Recomputing from [`brenda_js_tables_bulk_audit.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/brenda_js_tables_bulk_audit.csv) and joining to the NCBI crosswalk shows **22 distinct sample accessions / 63 observations**; page-row observations sum to 68 because two proteins have duplicate exact-sequence BRENDA records (4 + 1 observations duplicated). All 24 titles and headings matched the requested EC/organism/UniProt, and every page showed zero Substrates/Products, zero Natural Substrates, and zero References. The original four-EC FASTA collection result (21 proteins / 62 observations) remains correct for that narrower dataset; the dynamic-page union additionally included the separately found A0ACM7/CAJ88232.1 entry.

The subsequent GenBank→UniProt/UniParc crosswalk resolved 12/13 previously unmatched accessions, with 12/12 full sequences exactly matching NCBI FASTA. Two additional BRENDA sequence-index records were found: Q6HMK2 for AAT59229.1 and Q65D52 for AAU43012.1, representing 5 pilot observations. Their rendered exact-filter pages also showed 0 Substrates/Products, 0 Natural Substrates, and 0 References. Current dynamic-page total is **26 page rows / 24 distinct sample proteins / 68 distinct observations**; summed row counts are 73 due duplicate sequence entries. All 26 exact views had zero table counts. Full crosswalk evidence and boundaries are in [`2026-09-30-genbank-uniprot-brenda-crosswalk-update.md`](2026-09-30-genbank-uniprot-brenda-crosswalk-update.md) and [`genbank_uniprot_brenda_mapping_audit.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/genbank_uniprot_brenda_mapping_audit.csv).

As a positive control for the dynamic count method, the same process queried BRENDA's maize beta-glucosidase P49235 page. It exposed 18 Substrates/Products entries, 1 Natural Substrate, and 7 References; the result and check script are [`brenda_js_positive_control.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/brenda_js_positive_control.csv) and [`audit_brenda_js_positive_control.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_brenda_js_positive_control.py). This makes a generally broken zero-count parser less likely.

**Current bounded finding:** 68 source observations are not found in the 26 exact-protein-filtered BRENDA enzyme tables tested. This is a concrete candidate incremental-coverage set, but not a final “68 literature-unique negatives” claim: alternative EC assignments, aliases, other BRENDA views, and cited primary papers still need a bounded check. Twelve of the 13 proteins that had not matched the four downloaded EC sequence sets are now resolved through UniProt/UniParc; one accession (ABV62413.1) remains unresolved. A zero in BRENDA's exact view is not a measurement of publication bias or model utility.

## Reproducibility and evidence limits

- Heins source article: [DOI 10.1021/cb500244v](https://doi.org/10.1021/cb500244v); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4168791/).
- Sample record: [`gh1_nims_negative_sample_100.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/gh1_nims_negative_sample_100.csv), ID `GH1NIMS0001`.
- BRENDA species pages queried on 2026-09-30: [*Bacillus licheniformis*](https://brenda-enzymes.org/organism.php?organism=Bacillus+licheniformis) and 29 other sample taxa. For *B. licheniformis*, the normalized visible-text SHA-256 was `17644FFF4ED784462385591257F83815ACEC9B37974DB78AA9250D00BE33C042` on two successive requests; the page lists EC 3.2.1.86 as well as many other ECs.
- Crosswalk artifacts: [`audit_brenda_sample_taxa.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_brenda_sample_taxa.py), [`brenda_species_ec_crosswalk.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/brenda_species_ec_crosswalk.csv), [`audit_ncbi_accessions.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_ncbi_accessions.py), [`ncbi_accession_crosswalk.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/ncbi_accession_crosswalk.csv), [`compare_species_ec_crosswalk.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/compare_species_ec_crosswalk.py), and [`ncbi_brenda_species_ec_join.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/ncbi_brenda_species_ec_join.csv). The NCBI records were fetched from public E-utilities. Exact sequence-level BRENDA matches and assay-row matches remain unverified.
- Example of BRENDA retaining no-hydrolysis text: [Feng et al. 2009 record, BRENDA reference ID 702747](https://brenda-enzymes.org/literature.php?r=702747); BRENDA describes the source as *Properties of a metagenome-derived beta-glucosidase from the contents of rabbit cecum*.
- False-positive resolution for the page scan: BRENDA [reference 2075 under EC 5.1.1.13](https://www.brenda-enzymes.org/literature.php?e=5.1.1.13&r=2075) identifies the “no activity in strain IAM 10067” comment as aspartate-racemase evidence; its substrate table says “no racemase activity with Glu.” This is distinct from pilot GH1 accession CBL32986.1 / EC 3.2.1.86.
- BRENDA is at release 2026.1 (March 2026), and its public pages state CC BY 4.0. This audit used public web pages only; no account, SOAP credentials, or gated bulk download was used.
- Official SPARQL prototype description: [BRENDA news announcement](https://brenda-enzymes.org/news.php?news_id=2025-09-01-sparql); the endpoint was queried through its public API without credentials. Exact UniProt ID query outputs are preserved in `brenda_sparql_identifier_audit.csv`.
- Exact sequence identity cross-check: `audit_brenda_sequence_identity.py` compares NCBI E-utilities FASTA with BRENDA sequence pages; output `brenda_sequence_identity_audit.csv`. A parser bug affecting a short terminal sequence row was corrected before treating either match as verified.
- Classic enzyme-page check: the D4LC32-filtered [`enzyme.php`](https://www.brenda-enzymes.org/enzyme.php?ecno=3.2.1.21&organism%5B%5D=Ruminococcus+champanellensis&showtm=0&UniProtAcc=D4LC32) static response requires JavaScript to expose dynamic tables. Its empty References shell was recorded as unresolved, not as a no-reference result.
- JavaScript-rendered bulk exact-filter check: [`audit_brenda_js_tables_bulk.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_brenda_js_tables_bulk.py) queried 24 BRENDA protein records, while [`audit_brenda_js_positive_control.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_brenda_js_positive_control.py) validates the page counter against P49235's nonzero tables.
- This is a one-observation scoping check, not a representative coverage estimate, full BRENDA release audit, or proof of absence from every database.

## Next step

Expand the comparison beyond BRENDA taxonomy summaries by resolving exact sequence accessions to BRENDA/UniProt records where available, searching substrate/product fields and free-text commentary for each of the 100 observations, and manually verifying a stratified subset against BRENDA-cited original articles. Report exact accession+substrate hits, species+EC only, commentary-only, no observed match, and unresolved separately. Only after that can we estimate a database-unique fraction.
