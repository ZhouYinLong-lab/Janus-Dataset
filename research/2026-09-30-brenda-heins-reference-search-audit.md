# BRENDA reference-field search for the GH1 NIMS source paper

## Question and relevance

Does the public BRENDA full-text search show that Heins et al. (2014), the source of the GH1 NIMS pilot, is indexed as a reference—and can that alone establish whether its negative observations were curated?

Relevance: **3/3** to source provenance and database-coverage auditing. This is a bounded search audit, not evidence of assay-level coverage.

## Search method

On 2026-09-30, queried BRENDA's public full-text search (website release 2026.1) using `contains` for identifiers, title phrases, and names, plus its documented Boolean mode for narrowed author/year/topic combinations. Searches covered the complete database for the DOI and the dedicated Reference field (form field `tables[]=30`) for DOI, DOI suffix, PMID, title phrases, author-plus-year, author full name, initials, and surname. The official search help says full-text search can include comments, supports selecting individual fields, and reports a field-level hit count; the official download page states that bulk downloads require actively accepting the license, so this audit did not accept that agreement or download the licensed archive.

The reproducible query list and returned URLs/counts are in [`brenda_reference_search_audit.csv`](pilots/gh1-nims/brenda_reference_search_audit.csv), generated by [`audit_brenda_reference_search.py`](pilots/gh1-nims/audit_brenda_reference_search.py).

The 17 is still a surname-level field count, not a count of relevant enzyme assays or even a verified count of papers by the target author. The result-detail endpoint did not return within the bounded request timeout, so those 17 citations have not been inspected individually.

## Interpretation

This pass **does not verify that BRENDA indexes the Heins NIMS source paper**: stable identifiers and title phrases returned no hits, while the broad surname query returned 17 ambiguous reference-field hits. This is a stronger and more targeted check than an exact protein page with an empty reference table, but it remains a search result—not proof that the reference is absent from every BRENDA release, linked database, text-mining layer, or unsearched wording.

Most importantly, a paper-level reference hit would still not prove that BRENDA curated a specific `<0.1` enzyme–substrate observation with matching assay conditions. That requires record-level enzyme, substrate, source reference, and commentary/condition linkage. Conversely, a failed or zero search cannot make a pilot observation “database-unique.” Keep the relevant sample rows unresolved until a record-level match or a more complete, appropriately scoped search is available.
