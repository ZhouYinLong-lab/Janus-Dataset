## Direct evidence

### E001 — Failed experiments can carry predictive information

- **Claim:** Failed or unsuccessful experiments can be used as training observations for discovery models.
- **Source:** [Raccuglia et al., Nature (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17439)
- **Evidence:** The study used failed hydrothermal syntheses from archived laboratory notebooks, retained 3,955 complete reactions, and evaluated prospective synthesis recommendations.
- **Interpretation:** This is a strong proof of value for negative outcomes, but it is a materials-reaction case rather than a general molecular bioactivity result.
- **Confidence:** High for the specific case; low for generalization.

### E002 — Constructed negatives can encode benchmark bias

- **Claim:** A model can obtain strong virtual-screening performance by exploiting analogue or decoy bias rather than learning the intended molecular-recognition signal.
- **Source:** [Chen et al., PLOS ONE (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0220113)
- **Evidence:** The paper tested DUD-E and compared ligand-topology behavior with structure-based models.
- **Interpretation:** Synthetic decoys must not be treated as interchangeable with experimentally tested inactive compounds.
- **Confidence:** High for the DUD-E case.

### E003 — Measured inactive records and leakage-aware splits are already used

- **Claim:** Existing activity-model studies curate experimentally measured inactive records and use scaffold, structure, or temporal splits to reduce chemical bias.
- **Source:** [DEEPScreen, Chemical Science (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1039/C9SC03414E)
- **Evidence:** The study explicitly analyzes negative selection bias and builds target-specific datasets from ChEMBL activity records.
- **Interpretation:** A new project cannot claim novelty merely for collecting inactive examples or using scaffold splits.
- **Confidence:** High.

### E004 — Activity data are assay-dependent and labels are not globally interchangeable

- **Claim:** Compound activity datasets contain sparse compound-target observations, assay-specific distributions, measurement effects, and differences between virtual-screening and lead-optimization settings.
- **Source:** [Tian et al., Communications Chemistry (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-024-01204-4)
- **Evidence:** CARA organizes data at assay level and reports sparse labels, heterogeneous distributions, measurement/batch effects, and differences between task settings.
- **Interpretation:** Janus should treat assay and experimental context as part of the observation rather than as optional metadata.
- **Confidence:** High for the analyzed ChEMBL-derived data.

### E005 — Coverage bias is broader than class imbalance

- **Claim:** Widely used small-molecule datasets can under-cover parts of known molecular structure space.
- **Source:** [Kretschmer et al., Nature Communications (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-55462-w)
- **Evidence:** The paper proposes an MCES-based coverage measure and reports non-uniform coverage in common datasets.
- **Interpretation:** The AI-ready assessment should include representativeness and chemical-space coverage, not only positive-negative ratios.
- **Confidence:** High for the study’s coverage analysis.

### E006 — A directly overlapping inactive-compound resource exists

- **Claim:** InertDB is a recent resource explicitly built around biologically inactive small molecules.
- **Source:** [InertDB, Journal of Cheminformatics (2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11983867/)
- **Evidence:** The paper reports curated inactive compounds from PubChem and a generative expansion, with downstream model comparisons.
- **Interpretation:** Janus must differentiate itself from a database whose central contribution is simply a large inactive-compound collection. The strongest possible distinction is evidence-grounded, context-rich, observation-level negative data, including failures and qualitative evidence.
- **Confidence:** High that the resource exists; medium for the reported downstream gains until independently reproduced.

### E007 — Literature extraction is technically active but not yet standardized end to end

- **Claim:** Chemical literature extraction has expanding LLM capability, but evaluation, ground truth, and domain validation remain central problems.
- **Source:** [From text to insight, Chemical Society Reviews (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4CS00913D)
- **Evidence:** The review discusses precision, recall, false positives, false negatives, ground truth construction, and the bias toward positive and curated information.
- **Interpretation:** A Janus extraction contribution would need a small human-verified benchmark and source-linked evidence, not just prompts or an extraction demo.
- **Confidence:** High as a methodological landscape statement; the source is a review rather than a dedicated negative-evidence benchmark.

### E008 — AI-readiness is multidimensional and context-dependent

- **Claim:** AI-readiness includes provenance, characterization, explainability, sustainability, computability, and FAIRness; it is not a pass/fail property reducible to class balance.
- **Source:** [Clark et al., AI-readiness Criteria for Biomedical Data](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.10.23.619844)
- **Evidence:** The Bridge2AI criteria define seven dimensions and evaluate datasets with machine-actionable metadata.
- **Interpretation:** Janus should treat provenance and context as core schema fields and define readiness for a stated molecular task.
- **Confidence:** High as a framework; adaptation to molecular assays remains our work.

### E009 — Large-scale negative bioactivity modeling predates recent resources

- **Claim:** Negative bioactivity data have been incorporated into large-scale molecular target-prediction workflows for more than a decade.
- **Source:** [Mervin et al., Journal of Cheminformatics (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-015-0098-y)
- **Evidence:** The study combined more than 195 million ChEMBL and PubChem bioactivity points, selected presumed inactive compounds, and compared inactivity-inclusive models with active-only models using internal and external evaluation.
- **Interpretation:** Janus cannot claim that using negative bioactivity data in molecular ML is new. The unresolved issue is the scientific status and provenance of the negative observation, not the mere inclusion of a negative class.
- **Confidence:** High for the reported study; medium for generalization beyond target prediction.

### E012 — A direct but imperfect negative-inclusion ablation exists

- **Claim:** There is already published comparative evidence that adding negative bioactivity data can improve molecular target-prediction metrics; the broad value question is not untouched.
- **Source:** [Mervin et al., Journal of Cheminformatics (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-015-0098-y)
- **Evidence:** On an external WOMBAT set, the paper reports mean PR-AUC 0.56 vs 0.45 and BEDROC 0.85 vs 0.76 for negative-inclusive vs active-only models; Wilcoxon signed-rank p=4.96e-05 and 7.08e-08. Its negative-inclusive models combine PubChem author-annotated inactive assay outcomes with about 11 million sphere-exclusion “presumed inactive” compounds sampled from ChEMBL; the activity-only comparator is ChEMBL-active-only. WOMBAT itself lacks experimentally confirmed inactive records, and unannotated target pairs are treated as inactive for evaluation.
- **Interpretation:** This is meaningful author-reported evidence for a benefit in that target-fishing setup, but it does not isolate the effect of measured negatives from synthetic/presumed negatives, dataset scale/composition, or the test-label assumption. Janus should not claim “negative data's value has never been tested”; a defensible contribution is a stricter evidence-type and context-controlled replication/extension.
- **Confidence:** High that the paper reports the comparison and results; moderate/low that it identifies a causal benefit specific to experimental negatives.

### E010 — Open screening infrastructure can preserve both positive and negative pipeline outcomes

- **Claim:** An open chemical-biology infrastructure can store positive and negative activity data from primary and counter-screening assays.
- **Source:** [Škuta et al., ECBD, Nucleic Acids Research (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae904)
- **Evidence:** ECBD is designed as a FAIR repository for EU-OPENSCREEN data and explicitly includes primary and counter-screening results.
- **Interpretation:** The project must distinguish retrospective literature recovery from prospective data governance. “Preserving complete assay-pipeline outcomes” is an existing direction; Janus may add value by connecting such records with literature evidence, heterogeneous sources, and a cross-resource evaluation protocol.
- **Confidence:** High for the repository scope.

### E011 — Negative data have also been used to clean activity-related chemical signals

- **Claim:** Negative data have been used in statistical analysis of active-negative chemical correlations, not only as binary labels for classifiers.
- **Source:** [Lee et al., Ligand biological activity predicted by cleaning positive and negative chemical correlations](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/2a26b764-7e66-4db3-b5d0-2e41192c33c3)
- **Evidence:** The work combines high-quality negative data with a random-matrix-inspired framework to separate activity-related chemical differences from undersampling noise.
- **Interpretation:** The second-pass search must include statistical and representation-learning uses of negatives, not only dataset construction and literature mining.
- **Confidence:** Medium pending full bibliographic and full-text verification.

### E012 — Large-scale multimodal bioactivity extraction with downstream validation now exists

- **Claim:** A 2026 system extracts quantitative protein-ligand bioactivity from text, tables, figures, and chemical structures, and uses the resulting data in downstream modeling.
- **Source:** [Yan et al., BioMiner (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21508)
- **Evidence:** BioVista contains 16,457 expert-curated bioactivity entries from 500 publications. BioMiner reports an F1 score of 0.323 for complete bioactivity triplets, extracts 82,262 records from 11,683 papers for a pretraining application, and reports improvements in downstream evaluations.
- **Interpretation:** Janus cannot claim novelty for general large-scale literature bioactivity extraction or for demonstrating any downstream use. The remaining distinction must be specific to negative semantics, unknown-versus-negative separation, evidence quality, and controlled incremental-value experiments.
- **Confidence:** High that the preprint and benchmark claims exist; medium until peer review and independent reproduction.

### E013 — Strict splits continue to expose weak out-of-distribution generalization

- **Claim:** Bioactivity model performance can deteriorate sharply as test molecules move farther from the training distribution.
- **Source:** [Lee et al., Chemistry (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1002/chem.71208)
- **Evidence:** The study compares k-nearest-neighbor and modern ML models under increasingly strict splits and reports strong performance deterioration in out-of-distribution settings.
- **Interpretation:** Any claimed benefit from recovered negative evidence should survive structure-aware, assay-aware, or temporal evaluation and should be compared with a simple similarity baseline.
- **Confidence:** High for the reported study.

### E014 — Target-specific measured labels generally outperform untested-as-negative cross-target training, with target-dependent exceptions

- **Claim:** Molecular activity-model results depend on whether negative labels are measurements for the target assay or are inferred from the absence of a target-specific record; the preferred strategy varies by target.
- **Source:** [Pogodin et al., Frontiers in Chemistry (2018)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fchem.2018.00133).
- **Evidence:** Across 152 human kinases, authors compared per-kinase measured active/inactive training sets with merged training that labeled compounds lacking evidence for a particular kinase as “conditionally inactive,” plus a merged actives-only approach. The per-target measured sets generally had better classification and BEDROC; 13 kinases favored merged strategies for early recognition.
- **Interpretation:** This directly supports preserving the difference between “tested inactive” and “not recorded/tested” and warns that effects are target-dependent. It does not isolate the independent benefit of adding measured negatives while holding target, positives, sample size, class ratio, model and test split fixed.
- **Confidence:** High that the study reports the comparisons and aggregate findings; moderate as evidence for general molecular tasks because it uses a specific PASS/MNA workflow and cross-target training design.

### E015 — InertDB supports source-matched inactive selection, not a universal benefit from adding more negatives

- **Claim:** The negative-compound source can affect molecular screening performance, but InertDB results do not show a consistent improvement over assay-benchmark verified inactives.
- **Source:** [An et al., Journal of Cheminformatics (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-025-00999-1), full text and supplementary-linked results.
- **Evidence:** InertDB contains 3,205 curated inactive compounds (CIC) and 64,368 generatively produced compounds (GIC). In comparisons using the same held-out verified LIT-PCBA/MUV assay compounds, CIC/GIC source negatives outperform random PubChem/ZINC samples and DeepCoy decoys in some settings. In a separate replacement test, replacing benchmark verified inactives with CIC/GIC does not significantly change LIT-PCBA AUROC (CIC p=.52; GIC p=.85); MUV replacement with CIC gives a small significant improvement (p=.0032), which the authors relate to MUV's smaller training set.
- **Interpretation:** The paper weakens any blanket claim that increasing negative examples improves models. It shows source/chemical-space matching may matter; GIC are synthetic candidates, while CIC are broadly curated across PubChem assays and are not necessarily target-assay-specific measured negatives. The study does not test the incremental benefit of source-linked negatives newly recovered from papers beyond existing assay-specific observations.
- **Confidence:** High for the reported experiment and statistics; moderate for broad utility because evaluation uses ECFP4 random forests, AUROC/MCC/balanced accuracy and repeated random holdouts, not prospective, temporal, assay-OOD or calibration evaluation.

### E016 — Inactive-enriched docking models benefit from negative training data, but the benefit is confounded by negative-set size and task representation

- **Claim:** In a single-target PD-L1 structure-based virtual-screening study, inactive-enriched models substantially outperformed active-only baselines, but experimentally measured inactives did not consistently outperform a much larger random-decoy set.
- **Source:** [Gómez-Sacristán et al., Journal of Advanced Research (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2024.01.024).
- **Evidence:** The study compared PubChem AID 2316 true inactives, DeepCoy property-matched decoys, ZINC random decoys, and active-only training with two test sets. It reports classification PR-AUC <=.04 without inactives; among inactive-enriched models, true-inactive training was not significantly better than much more numerous random decoys, and using 23 times more random decoys improved SBVS on a true-inactive test set.
- **Interpretation:** This supports a role for negative-class chemical-space information in this docking-scoring task, but not an isolated causal advantage of experimentally measured status. Negative counts differ substantially; active-only classification redefines classes as high/low potency; features are docking-derived. Do not generalize directly to ordinary ligand-based bioactivity prediction.
- **Confidence:** High for the reported task-specific results; low-to-moderate for attributing the gains specifically to negative-label semantics.

### E017 — Inactive assay results appear less covered than active results in commonly used bioactivity resources

- **Claim:** Publicly released systematic pharmacology profiling data contain many drug-assay results absent from ChEMBL, DrugCentral, and subscription sources, with the gap largest for inactive results.
- **Source:** [Sutherland et al., Nature Communications (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-40064-9); [Zenodo SPD dataset v2](https://zenodo.org/records/8103950); [public analysis repository](https://github.com/Novartis/SPD).
- **Evidence:** SPD tested 1,958 drugs against 200 assays and summarized 147,653 concentration-response curves as 121,097 drug-assay pairs. Authors report ~95% of SPD results unique to compared resources, ~36% uniqueness for AC50<1 μM active results, and the highest uniqueness among inactive results. Among matched SPD/ChEMBL comparisons, 66% of SPD AC50≥10 μM records had ChEMBL median AC50<10 μM, while 82% of SPD AC50<0.1 μM records showed comparable potency in ChEMBL. A local field-based recomputation on the released file yielded 94.81% overall uniqueness and 37.60% for AC50<1 μM; this is a close sanity check, not exact replication.
- **Interpretation:** Strong direct evidence for *coverage/reporting disparity across selected resources*. It is consistent with publication bias but does not show that every SPD-unique observation was never published; authors note some can be found by manual literature search. Assay and entity matching differences are plausible alternatives. The public notebook inspected documents complex drug/metabolite, structure-match and assay-group selection but does not itself recreate the paper's uniqueness statistics. SPD is systematic panel profiling, not a historical full-text extraction dataset, and it does not test ML benefit from recovered negatives.
- **Confidence:** High for the authors' reported source-coverage comparison; moderate for its interpretation as publication bias; moderate for the independent approximate recomputation.

### E022 — Negative reaction records can aid a specialized low-positive reaction model, but evidence is not transferable to bioactivity negatives

- **Claim:** Unsuccessful reaction outcomes have demonstrated model utility under a specific reinforcement-learning setup; this is a neighboring-domain precedent, not evidence that literature-recovered molecular target inactives independently improve bioactivity models.
- **Sources:** [Toniato et al., Science Advances (2025), full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12164950/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adt5578); [Zenodo code](https://zenodo.org/records/15132405).
- **Evidence:** On real HiTEA HTE data, the low-positive setting reports validation positive accuracy 0.644±0.015 for RL versus 0.610±0.018 for fine-tuning, maximum 0.668 versus 0.628, and top-10 test positive accuracy 0.441 versus 0.427. At full positive coverage the reported averages are 0.891±0.001 versus 0.877±0.001. The negative class is defined as UV-area yield ≤1%; the authors state this cutoff was selected to test method applicability and discuss handling low but nonzero yields as future work. Only 9 of 130 high-yield products had a negative-yield counterpart from identical reactants. RegioSQM negatives are synthetically generated incorrect regioisomers; that setting is not experimental-failure evidence.
- **Interpretation:** This is a concrete demonstration that model value is conditional on the learning algorithm, data regime, and negative semantics. The modest HiTEA gain is entangled with a specialized RL reward model and does not isolate a universal effect of adding any negative data. It supports including cross-domain precedents in the review while keeping the central empirical test in a target-specific bioactivity assay.
- **Relevance:** 2/3. Direct evidence for negative-data utility in molecular reaction prediction; adjacent to, not direct evidence for, assay-specific negative bioactivity or literature extraction.
- **Confidence:** High for paper-reported setup/results and distinctions; no independent replication performed.
